OverviewWhy young people don’t go for walks, and what would get them out.

Walking feels purposeless when there’s no destination or errand attached.

Many young people don’t go out simply because walking feels purposeless when there’s no destination or errand attached. At the same time, they’re busy and often unable to commit to structured exercise routines.

Popular fitness trends tend to be high-effort and competitive, making them overwhelming to start and difficult to maintain. This leaves a gap for simple, accessible ways to stay active.

  • How might we

    How might we motivate the younger generation to stay active in order to maintain social and mental wellbeing?

  • Value proposition

    Stroll is the walk companion app that allows the productive age group to find purpose in walking by gamifying daily walks, turning walks into non-competitive entertainment.

  • Vision

    To make strolling the new scrolling.

  • Mission

    Turning screen time into real-world time, sparking true joy one stroll at a time.

What changed after testing

  • Change 1

    Less emphasis on activity tracking

    Multiple testers said activity tracking made the app feel like a health-improvement app, taking away the fun and making it feel dreadful to use. Despite it being one of the main features before, I shifted the focus from logging statistics to walking for fun.

  • Change 2

    Adding a community feature

    In the first pitch, the idea was to download Stroll galleries and post them on other social media to share with friends. Many testers said in-app connections with friends, to collaborate and motivate each other, would be motivating, so I added a Stroll community feature.

  • Change 3

    Entertainment first, not health first

    Unlike its competitors, Stroll is made to be a healthier form of entertainment for people who don’t like being active. Most health apps are competitive and focus on number-based improvement; Stroll takes that stress away as a starting point for a healthier choice.

07

ReflectionWhat designing Stroll taught me.

Key takeaways

  1. Understanding motivation beyond fitness

    Through the user interviews, I realized that people aren’t avoiding being active. They’re avoiding experiences that feel purposeless, or they don’t have enough time to squeeze in another activity. This pushed me to design features that use walking, a low-effort physical activity, and give it a sense of meaning.

  2. Designing for low-pressure engagement

    I learnt how to create gentle entry points that encourage consistency without adding stress or expectations, to avoid overwhelming users.

  3. Balancing gamification with calm

    This helped me practice using playful mechanics in a way that boosts enjoyment while still keeping the experience streamlined.

  4. Designing for real-life context

    I learnt to account for users’ concerns, like short attention spans, accessibility differences and preferences. Taking user feedback, I refined the interfaces to support the user experience.
